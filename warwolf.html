Warwolf :3

Assembly

Warwolf :3 is a floating-arm trebuchet Andy Truong and I built for a Pumps and Pipes competition. It develops an earlier Science Olympiad design I used in 2023. A vertically falling counterweight drives the arm and sling to launch a tennis ball; our longest launches were about 20 meters.

Team
Brian Lu & Andy Truong
Date
2023 (original design)
Technical ReportLaunch VideosOnshape

Arm

The arm pivots on bearings that travel along horizontal rails. This lets the counterweight fall vertically as the pivot travels along the rails. We used aluminum C-channel to reduce arm weight and printed containers to hold an adjustable quantity of lead fishing weights. An earlier arm failed with the channel opening facing upwards. We reversed its orientation in the final build so the closed face was on top.

Frame

The wooden frame keeps the counterweight and pivot bearings on their intended paths. Cross-bracing stiffens the structure, and the front plywood panel helps prevent the sides from splaying outwards. Aluminum channels provide contact surfaces for the moving parts, with lubrication applied before testing. A pin mounted at the rear holds the arm in its starting position until launch.

Sling

A fabric sling made from a facemask holds the tennis ball. Twine connects it to the arm through two keyrings: one stays attached, while the other slips off an angled pin during the launch. Bending that pin changes when the sling releases. Getting this timing right was essential, since a consistent arm motion alone did not guarantee a consistent launch.

Calibration and Alignment

Calibration launches showed how far the ball landed to either side of the aiming line. We used that offset to correct our aim and adjusted counterweight mass to change the range. The aiming scope and target were mounted on printed models of our club sponsor, Mrs. Taylor—the TaylorScope™ and TaylorTarget™. The names were a joke; the setup still gave us a reference for alignment.
